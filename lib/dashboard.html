      The load ladder

      Your top working set for each lift, week by week. Body weight comes down ~0.65kg a week. Upper body climbs ~2% a week, lower body ~4%, rounded to the plates the machine actually takes. Week 5 is a deload at 90%, week 7 is test week. Change a baseline and the whole ladder recalculates.

      How the numbers are worked out

      Research Women early in a lifting career (you're 16 weeks in) typically add strength faster than the long-run 0.5–1%/week, especially lower body; relative strength gains in women match men's. Lower body handles bigger jumps because the muscles are larger.

      My inference You added 5kg (10%) to lat pulldown in ~6 weeks while in a deficit, about 1.6%/week. Now you're eating more, 2% upper / 4% lower is a stretch, not a fantasy. Hit the target for 8 clean reps? Move up. Miss two weeks running? Repeat the week, don't drop.

      Body weight Losing about 0.5–1% of body weight a week while lifting and eating high protein is the range where muscle is best kept (moderate evidence). For you that's ~0.35–0.7kg a week, so the ladder aims for ~0.65kg/week: about 62.5kg by 22 Dec, with 60kg as the next goalpost in early February. Going faster than ~1%/week is where lean loss and stalled lifts start to show up.
